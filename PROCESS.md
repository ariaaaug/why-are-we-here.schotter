Tools Used
I used Gemini throughout the writing process of this essay. Specifically, I leveraged the AI for initial brainstorming, structuring the outline, translating my ideas into academic English, and refining the clarity of my arguments.
What I Kept
I retained Gemini’s conceptual framework surrounding "Describing vs. Specifying." This theoretical angle perfectly captured my personal experience with my interactive marine sketch. It provided a clear, convincing vocabulary to explain why natural language prompts fail to capture subtle interaction parameters—such as distance thresholds and trigger frequencies—and why designers still need code to achieve true precision.
What I Rejected
I rejected Gemini’s early draft paragraph on the history section, which oversimplified AI as a purely neutral productivity tool that completely replaces low-level syntax writing. I found this argument too surface-level and dry. I asked the system to rewrite it around Don Ihde’s phenomenology of technology and historical parallels like Georg Nees, emphasizing that tools actively shape how designers perceive creative possibilities rather than just saving writing time.
